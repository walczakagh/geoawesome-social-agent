## 1. Earth from Space: Namibia’s biggest salt pan
**Source:** [ESA Observing the Earth](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/10/Earth_from_Space_Namibia_s_biggest_salt_pan) · shortlist score 8/10 · Striking Sentinel-2 scene of Etosha's salt pan, a strong visual with a clear geo story on arid-landscape mapping.

<img src="https://www.esa.int/var/esa/storage/images/esa_multimedia/images/2026/10/earth_from_space_namibia_s_biggest_salt_pan/27564541-1-eng-GB/Earth_from_Space_Namibia_s_biggest_salt_pan_card_full.jpg" width="480">

**Image rights:** image can be used with credit
**Credit line:** contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2026), processed by ESA
**Licence on page:** CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO

**What it shows:** A Copernicus Sentinel-2 false-colour image, acquired on 5 April 2026, of Etosha National Park in northern Namibia, centred on the Etosha Pan salt flat (about 130 km east to west, 4760 sq km). Vegetation appears red because of Sentinel-2's near-infrared channel, with dense mopane woodland showing as dark red. Salt appears white, sediment-laden water light blue and clearer water dark blue. Water still in the smaller pans to the northwest, including the Natukanaoka Pan, shows in greens and blues. The image also shows the Ekuma river flowing south from Lake Oponono (about 1200 m) to the salt flat (1030 m).

**Check before posting:**
- The exact band combination used for the false-colour composite is not stated. The source says only that vegetation is red because of the near-infrared channel.
- Spatial resolution of the image is not given.
- Red patches on the pan itself only 'suggest' vegetation, according to ESA. The link to blue-green algae and salt-tolerant grasses is general context, not a confirmed identification in this image.
- Temperatures of 14°C to 30°C are typical averages for the start of the dry season, not measurements on the acquisition date.
- Wildlife and habitat details (flamingos, elephants, lions and others) are general background from ESA, not something visible in the image.

## 2. Sentinel-3C returns its first views of Earth
**Source:** [ESA Observing the Earth](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-3/Sentinel-3C_returns_its_first_views_of_Earth) · shortlist score 6/10 · Sentinel-3C first instrument returns, a concrete sensor milestone, though the post is more a status update than a visual story.

<img src="https://www.esa.int/var/esa/storage/images/esa_multimedia/images/2026/10/italy_and_beyond_imaged_by_sentinel-3c/27561379-1-eng-GB/Italy_and_beyond_imaged_by_Sentinel-3C_card_full.jpg" width="480">

**Image rights:** image can be used with credit
**Credit line:** ESA
**Licence on page:** not stated

**What it shows:** First images and measurements from Copernicus Sentinel-3C, about two weeks after launch. The lead visual is a natural-colour image from the satellite's ocean and land colour instrument, captured on 30 September. It shows Italy from southern Germany to Sicily: the Po Valley in brown and green, the snow-capped Alps, and lighter blue water along the Adriatic coast that ESA says is likely river sediment. Other first-light products are natural-colour views of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago (30 September) and Cuba and the Bahamas (29 September), a sea-surface height map from the synthetic aperture radar altimeter compared with the Copernicus Marine Service multimission sea-level anomaly, and Greenland Ice Sheet height tracks (30 September to 4 October) that closely match a reference digital elevation model.
